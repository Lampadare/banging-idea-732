The Problem

The cattle industry loses over $1 billion annually to problems caused by its own practices — and every existing technology can only monitor the damage. None of them can intervene.

01

Inflammation & Disease

Bovine Respiratory Disease (BRD) costs the US cattle industry billions per year. The primary driver is systemic inflammation. Current treatment: prophylactic antibiotics — which consumers and regulators increasingly reject.

02

Pre-Slaughter Stress

Cortisol release before slaughter causes meat darkening, toughening, and pH shifts that directly reduce carcass value. Stress management is a top farmer KPI with no neural-level intervention available today.

03

Digestive Efficiency

A cow is a meat factory where the engine is the digestive system. Feed-to-meat conversion ratio is the single biggest cost lever. The vagus nerve directly controls gut motility, appetite, and rumination.

The Vagus Nerve

The vagus is the longest cranial nerve in the mammalian body. In cattle, it fuses with the sympathetic trunk into a single cable — the vagosympathetic trunk — running alongside the carotid artery.

From Pig to Bovine

No published bovine vagus histomorphometry exists. We built a geometry pipeline that transforms real pig SPARC data into a realistic bovine vagosympathetic cross-section.

Ground Truth: 9 Pig Samples

Real fascicle positions from the SPARC-64 dataset (Pelot et al.). Each panel shows a different pig cervical vagus cross-section with 33–63 fascicles, providing the morphometric foundation for our bovine extrapolation.

PCA-Aligned Transform

We take a representative pig sample (P12-1, 57 fascicles), rotate it to PCA-aligned axes, then apply uniform ×1.25 scaling with elliptical wrapping (1.4:1 aspect ratio) to match estimated bovine dimensions. Fascicle diameters scale proportionally.

Bovine Vagosympathetic Trunk

The final geometry: 57 vagal fascicles (blue) + 42 sympathetic fascicles (orange, 15% of cross-sectional area). Sympathetic fibers are distributed throughout the trunk — not segregated — reflecting the fused vagosympathetic anatomy unique to ruminants. This is the core selectivity challenge.

Simulation Results

Nerve Cross-Section

Synthetic bovine vagus anatomy: 12 fascicles (blue) packed inside an elliptical boundary (4×3 mm), surrounded by an 8-electrode cuff (orange). Fascicle radii range from 50–300 μm, matching published pig vagus morphometry scaled to bovine dimensions.

Fascicle placement uses rejection sampling with minimum gap constraints to prevent overlap. The elliptical cross-section (aspect ratio 1.33:1) reflects the non-circular geometry observed in large-animal vagus nerve preparations.

Monopolar Thresholds

Activation thresholds vary dramatically by fiber type. Aα fibers (16 μm) activate at the lowest currents, while C fibers (0.8 μm) require orders-of-magnitude more current. This separation is the basis for selective stimulation.

Each panel shows threshold current (μA) per fascicle from Electrode 1. Fascicles nearest the active electrode have the lowest thresholds. The 15–20× threshold separation between A and B fibers defines the therapeutic window.

Recruitment Curves

The therapeutic window emerges between fiber populations. Aα motor fibers recruit fully below 0.5 mA. B autonomic fibers — carrying the anti-inflammatory pathway — recruit between 0.5–1.2 mA. C fibers remain inert throughout the therapeutic range.

These curves demonstrate that selective B-fiber activation is computationally achievable at the cervical level, validating the core premise of VagalBov: targeted anti-inflammatory stimulation without C-fiber side effects.

Therapeutic Targets

Priority 1

Anti-Inflammatory Pathway

~80% feasibility

The cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway (CAP) operates via B-fiber efferents → celiac ganglion → splenic nerve → α7nAChR on macrophages → ↓TNF-α. A parallel A-fiber afferent pathway via the NTS provides additional anti-inflammatory tone. Both pathways are activated by non-selective stimulation.

1.5–3 mA · 250 μs · 10–20 Hz · 30s ON / 5 min OFF
Priority 2

Gut Motility

~70% feasibility

B-fiber efferents in the dorsal/ventral vagal trunks project to the enteric nervous system. Cervically, these are interleaved with cardiac and laryngeal efferents. Subdiaphragmatic placement bypasses the selectivity problem entirely — GI fibers are already spatially segregated at that level.

1.5–4 mA · 300–500 μs · 5–10 Hz
Priority 3

Stress & Eructation

~30% feasibility

Eructation is a coordinated brainstem reflex driven by a medullary CPG. Open-loop stimulation can modulate frequency but cannot trigger on-demand eructation. Pre-slaughter stress reduction via vagal tone enhancement is more tractable and couples naturally with anti-inflammatory activation.

Our Process

How we built a selective VNS simulation framework in a weekend.

Research Deep-Dive

Comprehensive literature review of bovine vagus anatomy, fiber composition, activation thresholds, and EIT-guided selective stimulation. Key finding: zero published bovine histomorphometric data exists — every parameter must be extrapolated from pig or treated as a sweep variable.

Simulation Architecture

Designed a 7-stage pipeline: geometry generator → FEM solver → cable model → recruitment computer → selectivity scorer → parameter optimizer → sensitivity analysis. Built with NRV (Neuron Response Vue) and NEURON simulator backend.

Computational Validation

Generated synthetic nerve geometry, computed extracellular potentials, and ran threshold searches across all fiber types. Produced recruitment curves demonstrating a clear therapeutic window for selective B-fiber activation.

Anatomical Validation

Sourced and dissected sheep/goat cervical anatomy to validate surgical approach and identify the vagosympathetic trunk, carotid artery, and jugular vein in their true spatial relationship.

System Design

Designed closed-loop architecture: VNS cuff electrode + analog front-end + ADC for neural recording, with ultrasound-guided feedback for real-time stimulation protocol optimization.
